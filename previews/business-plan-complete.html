What I Love
Drawing, painting, pet portraits, watercolors
What I'm Good At
Capturing likeness, color mixing, working from photo references
What People Need
Affordable custom pet portraits, gifts that feel personal
How I Get Paid
Per-portrait pricing, $15-35 depending on size and detail
1 Vision
What We Do
Custom watercolor pet portraits for classmates and their families — hand-painted from photos, delivered framed or unframed
Why This Matters to Me
I've been drawing animals since I was 8 and people always say my portraits "look alive." I want to turn the thing I already do for fun into something people value enough to pay for — and every portrait I paint is a gift someone gives to a person who loves their pet.
The Golden Circle
WHY: Every pet owner wants to freeze a moment with their best friend. I believe art should be personal, not mass-produced. HOW: Hand-painted from the owner's favorite photo. WHAT: Custom watercolor pet portraits.
2 Customer
Target Customer
Girls at school whose families have pets — especially around Christmas, Mother's Day, and pet birthdays. The buyer is usually a parent paying for a gift, or a student buying for their own room.
Customer Profile
Sarah, 15. Has a golden retriever named Biscuit that she posts on Instagram constantly. Her mom's birthday is next month and she wants something personal but can't afford a $200 Etsy commission. She'd pay $25 for a hand-painted portrait if she could see examples first.
Revenue Model
$15 for a 5×7 pencil sketch, $25 for an 8×10 watercolor, $35 for 11×14 with background detail. Payment via Venmo or cash. Takes 3-5 days per portrait.
Interview Insights
4 interviews completed
Key insight: People don't just want a picture of their pet — they want the PERSONALITY captured. "Make him look goofy, that's how he really is." The emotional connection matters more than technical accuracy.
3 Competition & Differentiation
Research Your Competition
My main competitors are Etsy portrait artists ($60-200, 2-4 week wait) and phone filter apps (free but generic). My edge: I'm local, fast (3-5 days), cheap ($15-35), and I know the pets personally — I can capture personality, not just appearance.
Define Your Target Customer
I'm not competing with professional artists. I'm competing with the "I'll just print a photo at Walgreens" default. My customer is someone who wants something more personal than a photo but can't justify $100+ for professional art.
4 Brand
Brand Voice
Gut-feeling words: warm, personal, handmade, joyful, real
We are NOT: corporate, mass-produced, sterile, generic, "luxury"
Business Name
Elsa's Art Studio — tested with 5 friends over 24 hours. Runner-ups: "Paws & Paint," "Pet Canvas." Chose the personal name because customers are buying from ME, not a brand.
Visual Identity
Color: Warm cream (#FFF8F0) — feels handmade, not clinical. Font: Hand-written category — matches the hand-painted product. First impression: "This was made by a real person who cares about your pet."
5 Marketing & Sales
Zero-Budget Marketing
Three channels: (1) Instagram — post process videos of paintings, (2) school art hallway — display sample portraits with QR code, (3) word of mouth through friends who have pets. First action: DM 5 classmates with pet Instagrams offering a free sample portrait this week.
Social Media Strategy
1-1-1 rule: one process timelapse, one happy customer reaction, one behind-the-scenes of my workspace. Post 3x/week. Film the painting process on my phone propped against a book.
Word of Mouth & Referrals
Remarkable moment: include a tiny pencil sketch of the pet on the back of the portrait as a surprise bonus. Referral script: "Hey, I painted [friend]'s dog — she loved it. Want me to do yours? I have two spots open this week." 5 people to tell: Mom (she knows everyone), my art teacher, Sarah, Diego, Coach Kim (she has 3 dogs).
The Pitch
You know how everyone has a million photos of their pet on their phone but nothing on their wall? I paint custom watercolor portraits from your favorite photo — hand-painted, not printed — so you get something that actually captures their personality. Most people pay $25 and have it in less than a week.
6 The Numbers
Cost Per Portrait (8×10 watercolor)
Paper: $2 · Paints/water: $1 · Frame (if included): $4 · My time: ~2 hours
Total hard cost: $7 · At $25 price → $18 profit per portrait
Pricing Rationale
Etsy custom portraits start at $60. Local print shops charge $15 just for framing. At $25, I'm the only option between "free phone photo" and "$60+ professional art." The $15 sketch tier exists for friends who want something but can't spend $25.
Tracking System
Google Sheet: Date | Money In | Money Out | What For. Review every Sunday at 7pm. Two questions: Am I making more than I'm spending? What's my biggest cost I could cut?
7 Launch & First Sale
Shipping Before I'm Ready
MVP: one 5×7 pencil sketch for Sarah's dog Biscuit. Not watercolor, not framed — just the sketch. Perfectionism traps I'm ignoring: "I need a website first," "I should practice more breeds," "what if the likeness isn't perfect." Delivering Thursday after school.
First Customer Protocol
Confirmation: "Hey Sarah! I'll have Biscuit's portrait ready by Thursday. I'm working from that photo you posted last week — the one where he's sleeping on the couch. I'll text you when it's done." Risk #1: likeness is off → show her a work-in-progress and ask if she wants changes. Risk #2: she doesn't like it → offer a redo at no charge. Remarkable moment: include a tiny bonus sketch of Biscuit with sunglasses on the back.
Getting Feedback
Two questions: "Walk me through what happened when you showed it to your mom" and "If you could change one thing about the whole experience — ordering, waiting, the portrait itself — what would it be?" Pre-committed response to "it took too long": offer a rush option at $5 extra for 2-day turnaround.
After the First Sale
Celebration: photo of me holding the finished portrait → post on Instagram. 24-hour next action: DM three people who liked the post. Customer #2 targets: Diego (has a cat), my neighbor Mrs. Chen (just got a puppy), my art teacher (has two rescue dogs). Wisdom: "The hardest part wasn't painting — it was sending that first DM. Everything after that was just doing the thing I already know how to do."
